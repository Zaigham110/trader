Agents

Do the AI vetoes help?

Each time the strategy's rule wanted a trade, this records what the Strategy agent said and plays the signal forward as a code-only trade, counting each signal once and one position at a time. "Real trades" are this account's own closed trades. R means multiples of the amount risked. If the signals the agent declined would have lost, its vetoes are worth their cost.

Gold futures positioning

Large speculators' net position in COMEX gold futures (CFTC report). Slow weekly context shown to the Strategy and Risk advisor agents; it is not a timing signal and no code rule uses it.

What the agents have learned

Every time the rule wants a trade, the decision is graded against what the price then did. Once per trading day a review agent rewrites the playbook below from those measured numbers, and a strategy search tries about 24 rule variants (a few proposed by a research agent, the rest by an optimiser), scores each on four separate stretches of history and blind-tests only the best one; a variant that passes goes to the paper test, never straight to the account. R means multiples of the amount risked.

Research

Replay history through the rules, and the slow context the agents are shown.

Time machine

Replays the stored gold history, minute by minute, through the same strategy, session rules, risk limits and costs the live system uses. The AI agents are not replayed: asked about a past date, a model remembers what happened next, so its answer would not be honest. Their effect is measured on live decisions only.

Telegram signal channels

Signals from channels you follow, recorded the moment they are posted and played forward on our own prices with the real spread. R means multiples of the amount risked; the first target is the exit. Later edits and deletions are counted, not applied. A channel signal can never open a trade by itself: each fresh one is sent to you as an alert and shown to the Strategy agent as context with the channel's record.
